# Three obstacles for the Alpha prototype

Open `Assets/Scenes/Johnny/ObstacleLab.unity` and press Play, or choose **Obstacle Lab** from level selection. The level uses the existing player, light tracker, camera follow, death, victory, and menu systems. The tutorial and shared player prefab are unchanged. The lab has steady light coverage so the three obstacle behaviors can be learned separately; it does not replace teammates' light mechanics.

| Obstacle | Player decision | Feedback and initial tuning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spikes | Jump across a short hazard rather than walking or dashing into it. | Three red triangles; a matching polygon trigger. Two small clusters with safe landing areas. |
| Pulse beam | Wait for a safe interval, then cross. | `SAFE` / `GET CLEAR` / `STOP` labels plus dim / amber / red colors. 4 seconds safe, 0.85 seconds warning, 1.75 seconds active. |
| Crumble platform | Keep moving after landing; recover if a tile disappears. | Turns amber and says `MOVE`, collapses after 0.8 seconds, returns after 3 seconds once the player's body is clear. A permanent recovery floor sits below the bridge. |

Red hazards remain dangerous in darkness and while dashing. The large player trigger is only a light sensor: damage checks the actual player box geometry, so the sensor does not cause early hits. A beam continues detecting occupants while inactive and hurts a player who remains inside when it activates. Timers freeze during pause and reset on level restart. Crumbling is triggered only by a solid landing from above, not proximity, side contact, or touching the tile while intangible. The supplied crumble prefab is horizontal; keep it horizontal when placing it.

Reusable prefabs are in `Assets/Johnny/Obstacles/Prefabs`. Change timings on each instance in the Inspector. Scale/reposition spikes and beams normally; place crumble tiles at their intended top surface (their collider extends downward from the root). The editor command **Tools > Johnny UI > Create obstacle demonstration level** regenerates this demonstration and its three prefabs; close ObstacleLab first, and preserve manual edits before regenerating. Other open scenes are left untouched.

## Research and design rationale

- [Celeste's developer changelog](https://www.celestegame.com/changelog.html) documents spikes, electricity, falling blocks, and adjustments to hazard placement and collision checks. It supports a small precision challenge with accurate collision geometry; the pulse beam's warning cycle is our own adaptation, not a copied Celeste mechanic.
- [Nintendo's official listing for The Fall of Elena Temple](https://www.nintendo.com/us/store/products/the-fall-of-elena-temple-switch/) describes spikes and crumbling platforms among its puzzle-platform hazards. The short warning and recovery path here adapt that disappearing-support pattern to an early prototype.
- [Nintendo's Donkey Kong Country Returns developer interview](https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/wii/donkey-kong-country-returns/0/5/) discusses the appeal of varied platform movement and rewarding challenge. This informed introducing one rule at a time and allowing retries; it is design inspiration, not evidence that these exact obstacles are in that game.
- Unity references: [2D overlap queries](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.3/Documentation/ScriptReference/Physics2D.OverlapBox.html) and [trigger stay callbacks](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.3/Documentation/ScriptReference/MonoBehaviour.OnTriggerStay2D.html).

Sources were reviewed October 10, 2026. No external art, audio, source code, packages, or game assets were copied. The primitive sprites are generated by the editor helper. Cite Codex research, implementation, and verification assistance in the GDD External References.

## Verification

Original prototype verification before migration, Unity 6000.3.22f1 Play Mode verification on October 10, 2026: **21 tests passed, 0 failed** (11 menu tests and 10 obstacle tests). The route test traversed both spike clusters, waited for beam windows, crossed the bridge, and reached the actual victory trigger using the existing 5-unit walk speed, 20-unit jump impulse, and gravity scale 5. It drives the real player rigidbody deterministically rather than simulating keyboard input. Other tests cover safe idle spawn, accurate hurtbox contact including a disabled solid collider, safe beam crossing and activation on an occupant, landing vs. side contact, collapse/return without trapping the player, frozen timers on pause, and state reset on restart. Menu tests also verify Tutorial-to-Lab progression and the final level's replay/menu options. Local reports are in `tmp/ObstacleReview/tests.xml` and `tests.log`.

The original prototype Windows development build succeeded. Desktop checks confirmed both level cards, selecting and launching Obstacle Lab, readable introductory signs and spike visuals, pausing with Esc, and restarting from the pause menu. Beam and crumble behavior, and completing the full route, were verified by the physics tests. WebGL remains unverified because its Unity module is not installed locally.
